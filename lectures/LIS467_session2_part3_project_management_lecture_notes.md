## 1. Context: Your Team as a Web Design Company

In this class, your team acts as a **web design company** taking on a client website: gathering the requirements, then following the entire process of designing and building the site. Understanding how a **project manager** works — and managing the effort *as a project* — is essential to delivering on time.

---

## 2. What Is a Project?

🔑 A **project** is both **temporary** and **unique**:

| Property | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| **Temporary** | A definite **beginning and end** — not an ongoing operation |
| **Unique** | A **new, unfamiliar undertaking** producing a distinct product or service |

---

## 3. Requirements Gathering — Getting on the Same Page

⚠️ **Things can go horribly wrong** when requirements aren't managed. The classic cartoon: how the *customer explained it*, how the *project leader understood it*, how the *analyst designed it*, how the *programmer wrote it*, how the *business consultant described it* — all wildly different; barely any documentation; installation fails; the customer is billed for a roller coaster when they wanted a simple swing.

⭐ **Lesson:** Establish a **common ground and a common language** between you and the client so both of you understand what you're agreeing on. That is why it is critical to **document requirements**.

---

## 4. Project Success vs. Failure

### A project succeeds when:

- ✅ Customer requirements are **satisfied or exceeded** (exceeding is hard — customers often see the work and generate *new* requirements)
- ✅ Completed within the **allocated budget**
- ✅ Delivered **on time**
- ✅ **Accepted by the customer**

### A project fails when there is:

- ⚠️ **Scope creep** (see below)
- ⚠️ Unrealistic **planning and scheduling**
- ⚠️ Sloppy **requirements gathering**
- ⚠️ **Lack of resources** to complete the tasks
- ⚠️ **Lack of skills** required for the task at hand

### Scope creep

🔑 **Scope creep** = uncontrolled changes or continuous growth in the project's scope. It occurs when the scope is **not fully defined, not documented, or not well controlled**, and is generally considered harmful.

✅ **Managing it:** Scope changes are acceptable *if* budget, resources, and schedule increase along with the scope.

💡 **CIO anecdote (from the instructor's PhD days in Singapore):** when asked "can you add this to the project?", the CIO would always say **yes — but this will cost extra and take additional time.** Requirements can grow, as long as you have a mechanism to manage the growth: more people, more time, or more money.

## 5. What Is Project Management?

⭐ **Project management** is the application of **skills, knowledge, tools, and techniques** to meet the needs and expectations of the project's **stakeholders**.

💡 Its purpose is **prediction and prevention**, not reaction — plan well enough to foresee problems and prevent them from happening.

---

## 6. The Triple Constraint

🔑 Project management balances three interlocking constraints. Together they determine the **quality** of the project result:

| Constraint | Meaning | Class example |
|------------|---------|---------------|
| **Time** | Deliver within a given schedule | The class project must be delivered before the last class of the semester |
| **Cost** | Money and resources available | The budget the client provides |
| **Scope** | How much you're required to produce | e.g., a 5–8 page or 8–12 page website as agreed |

### Trade-offs — change one, adjust the others:

- Scope ↑ → time ↑ and cost ↑
- Time ↓ (tight deadline) → cost ↑ (hire more people) and/or scope ↓ (agree to drop requirements)
- Cost ↓ (tight budget) → ask for more time and/or reduce scope

---

## 7. Areas of Project Management

| Area | What it covers |
|------|----------------|
| **Scope management** | Ensuring *all* the work within scope — and *only* that work — is completed; defining how scope is set, who manages it, and how changes are vetted |
| **Issue management** | Handling issues (constraints to accomplishing project deliverables); issues that can't be easily resolved are **escalated**, logged, and **tracked to resolution** |
| **Cost management** | Controlling costs and how they are paid: **resources** (people, equipment, materials), full-time vs. contract staff, office space, estimated vs. actual costs, budget |
| **Quality management** | Quality assurance plan — testing (usability testing, other agreed testing) to ensure the project **conforms to requirements** and is suitable for use |
| **Communications management** | Agreeing how/when you report to the client: designated contact person, meeting schedule, email updates |
| **Risk management** | All projects should begin with an agreement on how risks will be handled: identify risks, plan **mitigation strategies**, maintain and update a **risk tracking log** |
| **Change control** | Managing changes to scope, specifications, and schedule — documenting and tracking each change |

---

## 8. Project Life Cycle

| Phase | What happens |
|-------|--------------|
| **1. Initiation / decision** | Deciding to undertake the project |
| **2. Definition** | Gathering requirements; defining what the project must deliver |
| **3. Planning** | How to proceed: hire people? learn skills? acquire resources? |
| **4. Implementation** | The actual work happens — with **usability testing at various levels** along the way |
| **5. Verification** | Confirm requirements are satisfied and the user signs off that the website is ready |
| **6. Closing** | Wrap up the project and move on |

---

## 9. Project Tracking Tools

| Tool | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Simple activity table** | Rows = activities of the project; columns = time (weeks required per activity, current status) |
| **Gantt chart** | Larger activities broken into sub-activities, showing time required for each and completion status (many free tools online — search "project management tools") |
| **PERT chart** | **P**rogram **E**valuation **R**eview **T**echnique — designed to analyze and represent the tasks involved in completing a given project; e.g., a network chart for a 7-month project with 5 milestones (10, 20, 30, 40, 50) and 6 activities, showing the time required for each |
| **Project schedule** | The documented timeline agreed with the client |

---

## 10. Key Terms Glossary

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Project** | A temporary undertaking (definite beginning and end) producing a unique product or service |
| **Project management** | Applying skills, knowledge, tools, and techniques to meet stakeholder needs and expectations |
| **Stakeholder** | Anyone with a need or expectation the project must satisfy (client, users, team) |
| **Scope** | The agreed amount of work/deliverables in the project |
| **Scope creep** | Uncontrolled, continuous growth of project scope without matching increases in time/budget/resources |
| **Triple constraint** | Time, cost, and scope — the three interdependent limits that determine quality |
| **Issue management** | Logging, escalating, and tracking problems that block deliverables |
| **Risk management** | Identifying risks up front, agreeing on mitigation strategies, and tracking them |
| **Quality assurance** | Testing processes (incl. usability testing) that ensure conformance to requirements |
| **Change control** | Documented management of changes to scope, specification, or schedule |
| **Gantt chart** | Bar-chart timeline of activities, durations, and completion status |
| **PERT chart** | Network diagram (Program Evaluation Review Technique) of project tasks and milestones |
| **Project life cycle** | Initiation → definition → planning → implementation → verification → closing |
