# LIS 467 — Session 5, Part 3: Mood Boards

**Course:** LIS 467 — Web Development & Information Architecture
**Topic:** What a mood board is, how it differs from a wireframe, what goes in one, and how designers use it with clients
**Source:** Panopto lecture transcript — "Session 5 Part 3 Mood boards"

---

## 1. What Is a Mood Board?

🔑 A **mood board** (also called an **inspiration board**) is a **compilation of inspirational elements** used by designers to **convey the mood** of a website and **express ideas at the beginning of a design project**.

- Mood boards are a very important part of the **design and look and feel** of a website.
- In practice, designers (in physical design fields) may collect **fabric swatches, colors, and objects** to capture the mood. For the web, the equivalent is digital.

### What goes in a mood board

- **Photography / images**
- **Color palettes**
- **Typography / fonts**
- **Patterns**
- **Text** samples
- Anything that conveys the **feel** you want

---

## 2. Mood Board vs. Wireframe

⭐ These are different tools for different questions:

| | **Wireframe** | **Mood board** |
|---|---|---|
| **Focus** | **Structure** — laying out *where things go* on the page | **Mood** — *what feeling* the site should convey |
| **Considers color/look and feel?** | No | Yes (that's the point) |
| **Considers layout?** | Yes | Not particularly |

---

## 3. The Question a Mood Board Answers

What mood should the site convey? For example:

- **Light and uplifting**
- **Dark/dramatic**
- **Serene**
- **Modern, edgy**
- **Soft, muted**
- **Colorful**

It is about thinking through **broad strokes** of the design before details.

---

## 4. Using Mood Boards With Clients

- Ask: **what mood does the client want** the site to convey? (The client is the person/organization that hired the web design company.)
- Mood boards are a form of **visual prototyping**: present **two or three different mood boards**, and the **client chooses** the one they like.
- They give a **group of ideas related to colors, fonts, and images** and get **feedback early**, before investing in detailed design.
- The visuals needn't be explained at length, but the intended **mood** should be evident from the board.

---

## 5. Examples and Resources

- The lecture showed a mood board for an **architectural site**, which feels very different from other boards.
- Searching **Google for "mood board"** returns many examples for different projects.
- Mood boards aren't limited to websites — they're also used for **interior design** and other design work.

---

## 6. Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Mood board / inspiration board** | A collage of colors, images, type, patterns, and text conveying a design's intended mood |
| **Visual prototyping** | Creating visual options early so clients can choose a direction |
| **Look and feel** | The aesthetic and emotional character of a site (Part 2) |
| **Wireframe** | Structural layout of a page without color/imagery (Session 4) |
