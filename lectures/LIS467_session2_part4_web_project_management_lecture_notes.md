## 1. General vs. Web Project Management

**General project management** applies to any sphere — construction, software, anything. **Web project management** applies those same disciplines specifically to building websites.

💡 **Reflection prompts from lecture:** Why should a web designer/developer bother with project management? What is involved in managing a web project? What prior project management experience do you have?

⭐ **Real-world example (ASIS&T website redesign):** The instructor served on the board when the Association for Information Science and Technology outsourced its website redesign. The project involved **over $100,000** and the winning team spent **~9 months** gathering requirements — talking to stakeholders about what was working, what wasn't, and what they wanted to see. Web projects for large organizations can be *big*.

## 2. Web Project Requirements (Gathered via Interviews)

| Requirement type | Questions to answer |
|------------------|---------------------|
| **Goals & audiences** | What is the site for, and who uses it? (typically established through interviews) |
| **Content requirements** | What content already exists? What should be removed? What needs to be added? How should it be structured and sequenced? |
| **Technical requirements** | What technologies/CMS will be used? |
| **Visual requirements** | Logo, color scheme, imagery |
| **Site structure** | Architecture and construction of the site |
| **Timeline** | Project schedule and deliverables |
| **Budget** | Cost estimates |

## 3. Usability — Design for People

🔑 **Make the website people-centric** — design from the point of view of the end user. Usability applies to *any* technology (even a pen: does it write well? feel right?); for a website it means:

- Is the **content useful**?
- Is the site **navigable**?
- Does it serve the end user — and the *various categories* of users?
- **How many clicks** does it take to get where you need to go?

### Personas

⭐ Rather than designing for "generic people," **envision a real person** accessing your website — a **persona**: a fictional but detailed typical user. Imagine their age, where they work, their requirements, and *why* they would visit your site. (Covered in depth in upcoming sessions.)

### Don't make users think or remember

- ✅ Keep it **simple** — users won't put effort into remembering how your site works.
- 💡 In the past, software shipped with help menus users were expected to read; modern design (e.g., Apple iOS devices) is **intuitive** — even children who can't read the language learn iPads by tapping. Aim for that.

### Use redundant, recognizable conventions

| Convention | Expected location |
|------------|-------------------|
| Menus | Top left (desktop); **hamburger menu** top right (mobile) |
| Contact info / social media links / address | Bottom (footer) |
| ⚠️ Menu at the bottom | Counterintuitive — avoid |

### Links

- ✅ Show **visited links in a different color** so users remember where they've been
- ⚠️ Links must **not look the same as plain text** — they should be visibly clickable

### Choices and response time

- Limit the number of items in groups of choices — don't overload one page.
- 🔑 **Response time matters.** Tolerance is proportional to perceived payoff: in dial-up days people waited minutes; now even a few seconds feels long.
- ⚠️ **~10-second rule:** with no feedback within about 10 seconds, users become impatient and may leave.
- ✅ Provide **progress indicators** (like installer/download progress bars) whenever an operation takes more than a few seconds, so users know how much longer it will take.

## 4. Using Cutting-Edge Technologies

| Poor reasons | Good reasons |
|--------------|--------------|
| Just to look cool | To **inform, educate, engage** |
| To prove that you can | To draw attention appropriately and enhance information |
| Because you have them available | E.g., JavaScript for specific behaviors, animation that genuinely enhances the user experience |

## 5. Accessibility

⭐ Accessibility is essential in modern web building — ensure your site meets **basic accessibility requirements**:

| Law | Requirement |
|-----|-------------|
| **Section 508, Rehabilitation Act (1986/1998)** | Entities doing business with the federal government must include solutions usable by employees with disabilities |
| **Americans with Disabilities Act (1990)** | Employers (15+ employees) must provide reasonable accommodations |

💡 Universities must also adhere to accessibility requirements. Later in the semester the course covers accessibility requirements and how to test your site against them (validators/checkers are available online).

## 6. Feedback, Proofreading & Validation

- ✅ Get feedback on your design from **other web designers**, **subject matter experts**, and different parties — on both content and design.
- ✅ **Proofread for grammar.**
- ✅ **Validate** the site for standards compliance (e.g., W3C validator) and run accessibility checks.

---

## 7. Maintenance Planning

Once the website is done, it must be maintained. Agree up front:

- **Who** will do the maintenance — will you hand it over to the client, or maintain it periodically yourself?
- **What happens if major problems occur** — keep **backup copies** of the site (e.g., in case of a virus/attack).
- **Scheduled periodic review:** Does content need updating? Is the design still working? Are there new tools you should use?

## 8. Key Terms Glossary

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Web project management** | Applying project management skills specifically to website builds |
| **Persona** | A detailed fictional profile of a typical end user, used to guide design |
| **Usability** | How useful, navigable, and user-serving a site is (incl. click depth) |
| **Hamburger menu** | The three-line mobile menu icon, conventionally top right |
| **10-second rule** | Users become impatient without feedback within ~10 seconds |
| **Progress indicator** | Visual feedback showing how long an operation will take |
| **Section 508** | Federal accessibility requirement for entities doing business with the US government |
| **ADA (1990)** | Americans with Disabilities Act — reasonable accommodation requirements |
| **Validation** | Checking a site against standards (W3C) and accessibility guidelines |
| **Maintenance plan** | Agreement on who maintains the site, backups, and scheduled reviews |
